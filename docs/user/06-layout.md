# Arranging the window

Every part of the window is a pane: New QSO, Worked before, Station, DXCC slots, Map, Log, FTx monitor, Cluster, Awards, Band map, Watch list, DXpeditions, Needed now, Contests, Propagation, Rotator, Notes and MyClubs. Some start as tabs behind others; click a tab to bring it to the front. Put them wherever suits your screen, and QRZero remembers the arrangement on this computer.

## Resizing

Drag the thin bar between two panes. Double-click the bar to share the space evenly. You can also click a bar and use the arrow keys.

## Moving a pane

Drag a pane's tab and drop it on another pane. While you drag, a blue outline shows where it will go:

- Drop near an **edge** of a pane to dock beside it: left, right, above or below.
- Drop in the **middle** to add it as another tab in that spot.
- Drop on a **tab strip** to put it among those tabs, where you let go.

## Hiding and showing panes

The **×** at the top right of a pane hides it. Show it again from **Layout** in the top bar, which lists every pane with a tick for the ones shown.

## Popping a pane out to its own window

The arrow button next to the **×** opens the pane in its own window (or right-click the pane's tab and pick **Pop out**), so you can put the FTx monitor, the cluster or the map on a second screen. Every pane except Log can pop out, including New QSO: put the entry form on one screen and the rest on another. The spots you click, the radio and the keyboard shortcuts work the same from the pop-out, and the QSOs you log show up in the main window. A QSO half-typed when you pop the form out or dock it back is cleared. Clicking a decode, a spot or a QSO in a popped-out pane works just as it does in the main window.

Close the window, or click **Dock back** (the arrow-into-a-box icon in its title bar), to put the pane back where it was in the main window (in the same tab group, or on the same side of its neighbour). QRZero remembers where each window was and reopens it there next time. When you close QRZero, the popped-out windows close with it.

In the desktop app, the **Always on top** button (the pin icon) in a pop-out's title bar keeps that window above your other windows (handy for a logging form over a full-screen radio program). The pin is highlighted while it is on. It affects only that window, and QRZero remembers the setting for each pane. A browser tab can't do this, so the button is not shown there.

In the desktop app, right-clicking where QRZero has no menu of its own does nothing, rather than showing a web browser's Back and Reload menu. Text boxes keep their Cut, Copy and Paste menu, and so does selected text. In a browser you get the browser's usual menu.

In a web browser, allow pop-ups for QRZero's page, or the window can't open.

## Frameless windows

The desktop app draws its own title bar: the top bar of the main window, and the bar along the top of each pop-out and of the QSO editor, take the place of the system frame. Drag an empty part of the bar to move the window, double-click it to maximize or restore, and drag any edge to resize. On Windows and Linux, minimize, maximize and close sit at the right end of the bar; on macOS the usual window buttons stay at the top left. Closing a pop-out this way docks its pane back, the same as **Dock back**.

To go back to the system frame, untick **Frameless windows** in **Settings**, **General** (or in the **☰** menu). On Windows and Linux every open window changes at once; on macOS the change applies the next time QRZero starts. Either way it is remembered. A browser tab always has the browser's own frame, so the option is not shown there.

## Saved layouts

Save arrangements you switch between, such as **Laptop** and **Shack, 2 screens**, or one for an FT8 evening:

1. Arrange the panes.
2. Choose **Layout**, **Save layout as…** and give it a name.

A saved layout also remembers which columns the **Log** tab shows, and their order and widths. That lets you keep, say, a wide **Searching** layout with extra columns for scrolling through the log, and a plain **Operating** one for everyday use. Layouts saved by an older version don't include columns; loading one leaves your current columns as they are, and saving it again adds them.

Pick a saved layout from **Layout** to switch to it, popped-out windows included. **Reset to default** goes back to the starting arrangement. Layouts, like your column choices, filters and other window settings, are kept in your log database, so they survive restarts and updates and come back with a restored backup.

**Lock panes** stops tabs being dragged or hidden by accident. You can still resize panes while they are locked.
